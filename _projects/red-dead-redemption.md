I was a technical designer on Red Dead Redemption, focused on ambient systems — the open world as it behaves when you are not looking at a mission beat.

### What I owned

I was the primary designer on the in-house animation state-machine tool. That meant the graphs that drove how things moved and reacted: player, AI, missions, animals, NPCs.

- More than 600 state machines across those uses.
- Animals and NPC routines (bartenders, blacksmiths, and other world characters) that used props, triggered sound and particles, and could be interrupted by the player without popping out of animation.
- Day-to-day work with animation programming to extend the tool when the design needed something the current build could not do.
- Mentoring two QA testers into design: trained them on the state-machine work and oversaw their contributions.

The production lesson is still the one I use: a tool that only a specialist can operate will not survive a content-heavy ship. Making the state machine usable, then teaching other people to work in it, was as much of the job as authoring the graphs themselves.
